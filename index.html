Areas of Legal Practice

Probate & Estate Administration

Legal representation and advisory services for executors, administrators, and beneficiaries pursuant to the laws of Trinidad & Tobago.

Grant of Probate

Applicable where the deceased person left a valid Last Will and Testament appointing an Executor. Legal services encompass proof of testamentary capacity, attestation affidavits, estate asset inventories, and formal lodging at the High Court Probate Registry.

  • Verification of Testamentary Execution
  • Gazette & Registry Public Notices

Letters of Administration

Where a person dies intestate (without leaving a valid Will), administration of the estate is governed by the Administration of Estates Act, Chap. 9:01. We advise on statutory priority of next-of-kin entitlement, prepare oaths and administration bonds, and obtain consents or renunciations.

  • Determination of Statutory Entitlement
  • Preparation of Consents & Renunciations

Resealing Foreign Grants

Where grants of probate or letters of administration have been obtained in a Commonwealth or designated foreign jurisdiction, an application may be made to reseal the grant in the High Court of Trinidad and Tobago to deal with local real property or accounts.

  • Resealing Under Statutory Provisions
  • Representation via Power of Attorney

Deeds of Assent & Conveyance

Following the grant of representation, legal title to real estate must be formally vested in the rightful beneficiaries. Chambers drafts and registers Deeds of Assent and transfers under both Common Law and Real Property Act (RPA) titles.

  • Title Search & Due Diligence
  • Registrar General's Department Registration

Caveats & Contentious Matters

Lodging caveats at the High Court Registry to prevent grants being issued without notice, appearance to warnings, and legal representation concerning disputes on validity, undue influence, or testamentary capacity.

  • Registry Caveat Lodgement & Warnings
  • Contentious Estate Advisory

Preparation of Wills & Codicils

Drafting testamentary instruments in strict compliance with the formal execution requirements of the Wills and Probate Act, Chap. 9:03, including appointment of executors, testamentary trusts, and specific devises of real and personal property.

  • Compliance with Statutory Formalities
  • Structured Devises and Residuary Bequests

    High Court Probate Registry Procedure

    General progression of a contentious or non-contentious estate application in Trinidad & Tobago.

    1

    Document Collation

    Gather certified death certificate, title deeds, financial balances, valuations, and birth or marriage records establishing entitlement.

    2

    Statutory Drafting

    Counsel settles statutory affidavits (Form 1 or Form 5), schedules of assets and liabilities, and conduct of preliminary caveat searches.

    3

    High Court Lodgement

    Formal filing through the Judiciary e-filing system, followed by the requisite statutory advertisements in the Gazette and daily newspapers.

    4

    Grant Issuance

    Following examination of title and resolution of any requisitions from the Registrar, the Grant under seal of the Supreme Court is issued.

    5

    Vesting & Assent

    Closing of accounts, liquidation of statutory liabilities, and preparation of registered Deeds of Assent to convey real property.

    Frequently Asked Questions

    Information regarding estate administration under the laws of Trinidad & Tobago.

    Virtual consultations are conducted via secure video conferencing platforms (Zoom or Microsoft Teams). Clients may transmit scanned copies of primary testamentary or estate documentation prior to the scheduled meeting for review. Counsel provides legal advice on the statutory requirements, procedural sequence, and documentation needed to file in the Supreme Court.
    Under the Wills and Probate Act, Chap. 9:03, where the gross value of the personal and real property exceeds $4,800.00 TT, formal application for a Grant of Probate or Letters of Administration must be prepared and filed in the High Court by an Attorney-at-Law admitted to practice in Trinidad and Tobago.
    Processing duration at the Probate Registry is contingent on Registry caseloads, statutory notice periods, and whether queries are issued regarding affidavit evidence or title. Timelines are optimized when initial inventory documents, certified death records, and title deeds are accurately prepared at first filing.
    Commercial banks in Trinidad & Tobago maintain accounts in frozen status upon receipt of notice of death until presented with a sealed office copy of the Grant of Probate or Letters of Administration. Discretionary releases for verified funeral disbursements remain subject to individual banking institution policy.
    Beneficiaries or named executors residing in foreign jurisdictions may execute powers of attorney, statutory declarations, or renunciations before a Notary Public in their jurisdiction of residence. Such documents may then be authenticated and submitted to the High Court of Trinidad and Tobago.
